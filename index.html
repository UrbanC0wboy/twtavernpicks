How this was built, and where it is soft

Historical P&L comes from equity curves, not summed positions

Polymarket's /positions endpoint returns resolved-but-unredeemed positions alongside live ones, and winners disappear from it once redeemed while worthless losers linger indefinitely. Summing profit across that list is severely biased — for one wallet here it implied a −$8.9M loss against $198k of actual exposure. Lifetime figures therefore come from the daily equity curve API, and /positions is used only to read what is open right now, filtered to markets still trading between 0 and 1.

"Lifetime" means 199 days, not all time

The equity-curve API returns roughly 199 days of daily history (from 2026-03-20), so every lifetime figure is bounded by that window. A wallet that made its money before then will understate. Max drawdown is peak-to-trough within the same window.

The sports leaderboard is a short rolling window

Ranking comes from Polymarket's own category=sports leaderboard, which reflects a short recent window rather than all-time performance. That mismatch is the point of the page, but it does mean the ranking will look different tomorrow.

Sport classification is tag-based

Each position is classified by its Gamma event tags, falling back to slug prefixes when an event is untagged. Markets tagged only with a league and not with sports are still captured; genuinely untagged exotic markets may be missed.

Cross-venue gaps are timing, not free money

Polymarket prices are read at the snapshot time above; Kalshi prices are that market's last trade. On an in-play match both move fast, so a large gap usually reflects the minutes between the two reads rather than a real arbitrage. Matches are joined on the pair of players plus the date encoded in the Kalshi ticker — joining on a single player name silently pairs live matches against ones that settled weeks earlier.

"Consensus" means consensus among these 25 wallets

Shared trades are computed across the profiled wallets only, not the whole exchange. Two of them on the same side is a coincidence as often as a signal, and several may be following the same public model or belong to one operator. Markets are grouped by condition ID, so both sides of one question group together — that is what makes the disagreements above detectable.

Persistence needs repeated runs to mean anything

Each run appends every ranked wallet's cumulative P&L to snapshots.jsonl. Differencing that between runs gives what a wallet earned in the gap, which is resistant to the one-good-day effect that dominates the leaderboard. It requires at least two runs spaced far enough apart to contain real trading, and it only tracks wallets that keep appearing in the top 50 — a wallet that drops off the board stops being sampled, so long-run figures are survivor-weighted.

Position sizes are visible; intent is not

A wallet may be hedged elsewhere, market-making rather than betting a view, or one of several controlled by the same person. Public wallets show what was traded, never why.
